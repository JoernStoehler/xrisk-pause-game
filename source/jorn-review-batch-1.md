## 1. Existing frontier inference and the opening political bargain

All versions immediately stop prohibited frontier training, put monitors with
stop authority at obvious sites, move to factual custody where workload assurance
is weak, inventory chips and supply chains, and consolidate covered clusters.

**Cold version.** Frontier weights and services are powered down or cold-held.
This removes cheap AI-assisted experiments, algorithm research, dangerous
operational help, cover traffic, and weight/credential attack surface. It also
withholds already demonstrated useful technology.

**Restricted version.** Hosted inference continues only for named users and
tasks, with price/rate/tool/memory restrictions, logs, no local weights, no
successor-training use, and shutdown where inference cannot be distinguished
from training. Important industry and government uses may remain while careless
capability-research teams lose cheap access.

**Broad version.** Hosted frontier inference remains widely useful. It maximizes
current economic benefit but also experiment throughput, algorithmic progress,
dual-use leakage, cover workloads, and pressure for exceptions.

The corrected political mechanism is not simply “inference creates treaty
durability.” It is more specific:

`inference ban -> affected firms and users argue that the pause withholds useful,
already-demonstrated non-ASI technology -> the defense must shift from preventing
direct accidental ASI to the more inferential claim that inference accelerates
research toward ASI -> some chance public/elite opinion and political actors move
toward industry interests -> pressure to weaken the pause`

Allowing inference removes or weakens that criticism and makes “no new large
training runs” a simpler public red line, while creating the technical hazards
above. The bargaining sequence may matter: begin with an inference ban and later
trade it away as a concession, or avoid provoking the conflict in the first
place. Which is better depends on early institutional capacity, the political
climate, and whether companies mobilize opposition immediately.

**Weak empirical anchor.** The June 2026 Fable 5 restriction lasted a little over
two weeks. Publicly visible opposition was concentrated in Anthropic, AI/tech
interests, affected enterprise users, and European technological-sovereignty
politics; the accessible reporting does not establish broad US mass-public
outcry. The restriction ended through a negotiated safeguards/government-
collaboration package. This weakly supports low immediate public resistance to
a targeted rollback, but it is a poor analogue for a durable pause: Fable was
new, the legal order targeted foreign access, Anthropic implemented it as a
complete shutdown, and the dispute concerned cyber misuse rather than research
acceleration toward ASI.

**Review request:** Which arrows are missing or reversed? Which existing-model
uses dominate dangerous research acceleration, on roughly days/weeks, months,
or years? Is restricted hosted inference a stable middle state? How much does
the bargaining sequence matter compared with proactively choosing the likely
compromise?

## 2. Three cognitive-reinvestment routes and carriers

Hold the starting model's capability profile fixed. These may be successive
stages of one path rather than mutually exclusive hypotheses.

**A — internal procedure.** During an allowed bounded research session, one live
context discovers a better way to think. It neither edits weights nor writes a
program. The gain may vanish with context, persist through long-term memory, be
expressible to another copy, or help discover a durable change. Only the running
service and perhaps unusual outputs are externally visible.

**B — explicit theory.** The model develops an explicit theory of machine
learning, writes code and experimental predictions, and humans or other models
can examine and transmit it. The insight is communicable before implementation;
training a changed model leaves larger compute and artifact traces. Evaluation
may still be wrong or manipulated.

**C — successor.** A lab implements and trains a new research model with greater
serial coherence, research taste, or speed. The improvement is durable and
copyable, but depends on training resources, credentials, evaluators, and an
implementation window. Later generations may shorten that window or fizzle on
a new bottleneck.

The model treats context-local, communicated, scaffold-persistent, and
successor-inherited changes as fallible transitions. It separately compares time
to the next implemented improvement with the lab's stop time and ISIA/state
response time.

**Review request:** Are these the right distinctions? Which transformations are
strongly coupled, and what evidence would they leave? When does comparing the
loop with lab or ISIA response become conceptually wrong because the system can
already acquire access, manipulate review, or improve within one authorized
inference episode?

## 3. What a technically easy winning world contains

The game needs some sampled worlds in which humanity can win, but should not
define them as “all checklist items happened to be true.” Candidate route
families:

**Designed cognition.** Humans, uploads/enhanced humans, or tools develop an
architecture whose strategically relevant cognition, objective realization,
reflection, and successor process are understood by construction rather than
selected opaquely through frontier training. Hard residual questions may include
value/ontology choice, corrigibility, implementation fidelity, and deployment.

**Control-assisted learned system.** A learned model remains opaque in important
ways, but restricted tasks, monitoring, trusted components, anti-collusion,
security, and staged handoff prevent it from defeating the process while it
helps produce safety work. The main concern is that bounded-trial evidence stops
applying before or during recursive improvement.

**Enhanced-human route.** A large amount of cognition still recognizable as
human—uploads, intelligence enhancement, or both—does the theory/design work.
This changes research speed and perhaps verification, while introducing its own
alignment, governance, and capability-leakage problems.

For every route, separate (i) objectively adequate, (ii) humanly recognizable
as adequate before the irreversible step, and (iii) legally authorized and
actually implemented. A safe-but-unrecognizable design can leave a good
institution paused indefinitely; a persuasive false case can kill everyone.

**Review request:** Which routes may be objectively winning under your default
model, which should appear only as tempting false proposals, and what route is
missing? For any real route, what observation could give a competent institution
a justified substantial positive update before the decisive build? “None;
success is only lucky” is also an architecture-defining answer.

## 4. Treaty failure while leaders understand the catastrophe argument

**War trajectory.** US and Chinese top leaders continue to believe that an
unsafe ASI run is not a usable weapon because it soon kills everyone. A Taiwan
war nevertheless evacuates inspectors, closes raw-data channels, reallocates
power and chips, activates military emergency authority, destroys or seizes
monitored sites, and lets security organizations exclude civilian implementers.
Neither leader orders a race; operational restraint becomes unverifiable and
some subordinates gain the means to proceed. Remote evidence, disabled banks,
multi-party credentials, and military continuity preserve some paths but not all.

**Deliberate-weakening trajectory.** A leader continues to assent explicitly to
“ending the pause causes near-term extinction,” has authority and a physically
available restraint option, but still weakens it because of Taiwan, economic
pressure, national humiliation, a five-pro-versus-four-con decision process, or
an emergency heuristic. This differs from changing the extinction belief,
believing there is safe headroom, losing implementation control, or never seeing
the relevant information.

**Review request:** Is the war trajectory missing a major technical or
organizational path? Is deliberate weakening a real structural family, or should
apparent cases always decompose into changed/conditional belief, information
failure, limited control, subordinate action, preference aggregation, or ordinary
mistake?
