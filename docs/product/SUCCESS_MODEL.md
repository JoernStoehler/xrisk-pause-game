# Success model

Status: product decision model, 11 July 2026. This is a challengeable account of
what success would mean and what evidence could justify stronger claims. It is
not Jörn's approval of domain content, evidence that the game works, a release
decision, or a forecast that a treaty will be established. Its product
priorities have not been revalidated since July.

## The outcome and the route

**Terminal objective:** humanity survives the century.

The project's intended contribution is narrower than that objective:

```text
a readable, compelling Director game
  -> sustained attention and felt agency
  -> better understanding of treaty operation under uncertainty
  -> transfer to unfamiliar claims and decisions
  -> use in organizing, teaching, briefing, critique, or policy design
  -> better public and elite reasoning about credible proposals and objections
  -> more capable pressure, coalition-building, design, establishment and operation of an adequate treaty
  -> less unsafe development of superintelligence
  -> a better chance that humanity survives the century
```

Every arrow after transfer is a long-range product hypothesis or mission theory
of change, not an observed product effect. The game can contribute without being
sufficient, and treaty success can depend on conditions the game cannot affect.
Reach, recall, completion, or institutional adoption must never be reported as
survival impact.

The Director scenario begins after founding, so it addresses operation more
directly than formation. The formation bridge is the hypothesis that operational
literacy makes adequate proposals more credible, defensible, and actionable for
organizers, educators, analysts, officials, and other politically relevant
actors. Those roles affect different decisions and must not be pooled as one
“policy audience.”

The causal content inside play is similarly explicit:

```text
decision -> authority -> implementation -> factual control
         -> evidence -> interpretation -> response -> delayed consequence
```

This chain exists because the distinctive public lesson is that an agreement is
not automatically control and a pause is active crisis management. If target
humans already make these distinctions reliably, or if play does not improve
their reasoning about unfamiliar cases, this teaching strategy weakens.

## What is fixed, judged, hypothesized, tested, or observed

| Status | Current item | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fixed product premise** | Surviving the century is the terminal objective. Target players grant that superintelligence is possible and lethal if not safely developed. | Do not spend the core game re-litigating extinction risk or stage a fake neutral race-versus-pause contest. |
| **Fixed scenario premise** | US and Chinese leaders found a ban treaty for that reason; ISIA exists; the player is its Director and wants it to succeed. | Test how to establish and operate adequate control, including serious objections and failure, without pretending continued unsafe development is an equally valid objective. This is not a forecast that founding is easy or imminent. |
| **Expert judgment** | Jörn judges that without an adequate pause humanity is almost certainly dead. | Treat this as a directional, attributed judgment—not a playable probability, diagnostic frequency, countdown, or meter. Jörn still owns approval of expert-grounded player content. |
| **Fixed architecture premise** | Mobile simplicity is architectural: one focal decision, a small known-state surface, and progressive disclosure. | Complexity must be compressed in the model and prose, not merely squeezed into smaller type. |
| **Product hypotheses** | Director identification, consequential choices, receipts, callbacks, retrospectives, and replay can create pleasure, comprehension, transfer, and useful reasoning. Premise-qualified audiences and facilitators may carry that reasoning into discourse or policy contexts. | Each claim remains defeasible until the relevant human or field evidence exists. |

## What a successful final product must accomplish

A successful final product must satisfy all of these dimensions together. A
strong result in one cannot silently compensate for failure in another.

| Dimension and criterion | Why it exists | Observation that would weaken or reverse it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mission and model fidelity:** play makes treaty adequacy and operation causally legible without fake neutrality or magical institutional control. | The game exists to improve reasoning about a potentially survival-relevant intervention, not merely to wear its setting. | Players learn that signing, monitoring, or a Director's order automatically controls hardware; or the most engaging design requires reopening extinction as a balanced lifestyle choice. |
| **Claim and epistemic integrity:** truth, observation, evidence, belief, finding, authority, implementation, and retrospective knowledge remain distinct; uncertainty and claim status are honest. | False confidence about verification or safety could make discourse worse, while unlabelled fixtures turn playability choices into apparent forecasts. | Players infer omniscience, treat branch frequency as likelihood, or repeat a compressed claim that Jörn or sources reject. |
| **Mobile readability and editorial simplicity:** the primary path is comfortably readable on real small phones at ordinary settings, with only decision-relevant text in the moment. | Mobile-first is an access and product-shape constraint; unreadable correctness has no public effect. | Target users strain, skip, zoom, lose the focal action, or describe the experience as dense even though it technically fits. The current public UI already supplies this counterevidence. |
| **Visual craft and appeal:** palette, typography, imagery, portrait treatment, spacing, composition, motion, and finish create a coherent, inviting Director fantasy at mobile scale. | First impressions affect trust, desire to continue, sharing, and whether the artifact feels like a game worth recommending rather than an internal policy dashboard. Readability alone cannot establish this. | Target users describe the artifact as ugly, generic, amateur, tonally wrong, visually incoherent, or not worth sharing even when they can read and operate it. The current public UI already supplies this counterevidence. |
| **Interaction and accessibility:** players can perceive, navigate, understand, and act through supported input and assistive modes without losing essential state. | Excluding people or making consequential actions error-prone undermines reach, evidence quality, and the Director fantasy. | Real keyboard, screen-reader, low-vision, limited-dexterity, touch, or device use blocks decisions or changes the information available. |
| **Game feel and agency:** choices create understandable leverage, uncertainty, recovery, and delayed consequences; players experience pleasure beyond being taught. | Attention and voluntary continuation are required for the mechanism model to become felt knowledge rather than homework. | Players see a fixed assay, opinion quiz, paperwork, arbitrary punishment, or choices whose visible outcomes barely differ. |
| **Narrative and emotional appeal:** people and crises make the institution worth caring about without turning advisors into truth meters or using extinction as empty spectacle. | Emotion supports attention, memory, sharing, and identification with active crisis management. | Players feel detached, manipulated, fatalistic, or unable to recount a consequential human story from play. |
| **Cold first use:** premise-qualified newcomers quickly understand who they are, what they want, what they can do, and why the first decision matters. | A product cannot rely on a facilitator, repository context, or designer explanation unless facilitation is the selected distribution form. | Silent first-use players search for the objective, hidden anti-treaty route, glossary, or instructions before meaningful action. |
| **Comprehension and transfer:** players explain the causal route of an outcome and reason better about novel treaty claims, including limits and competent objections. | Recall of game vocabulary is not improved public reasoning. Transfer is the first link that directly supports the intended external impact route. | Gains disappear on unfamiliar cases, amount to slogan recall, or create confident new misconceptions. |
| **Judgment quality:** transferred distinctions support proportionate, threat-model-sensitive choices and serious evaluation of capture, rights, sovereignty, escalation, false assurance, and safe exit. | A player may name every layer yet become more confident in an inadequate or harmful package. | Players optimize toward the authored institution, treat one replayed policy as generally optimal, or discount competent objections because they impede the Director. |
| **Reach, sharing, and influence:** the product reaches premise-qualified people and supports qualified referrals or repeated institutional use; downstream policy relevance is measured separately from exposure. | Better reasoning cannot contribute if the artifact is neither encountered nor used where discourse and policy are shaped. | Visits and share intents rise without qualified starts, transfer, recipient engagement, reuse, or policy-context use; facilitation, not the game, explains all value. |
| **Engineering and content operations:** behavior matches the symbolic model, works reliably on supported devices, protects epistemic boundaries, and can be authored, reviewed, versioned, and maintained without claim drift. | A causal game is a maintained evidence system; regressions can silently change both play and public claims. | Code and model diverge, hidden truth leaks, telemetry is unsafe, performance blocks use, or adding reviewed episodes causes duplicated claims and unreviewable callbacks. |

These criteria are intentionally challengeable. A criterion should change when
better evidence shows it is unnecessary for the impact route, conflicts with a
more important constraint, or cannot discriminate a useful product from a bad
one. It should not survive merely because it is written here.

## Failure modes and proxy traps

- **Conformance as approval:** passing semantic, accessibility-automation,
  screenshot, build, or deployment checks while the human experience remains
  unreadable, dense, confusing, or dull.
- **Legibility as visual approval:** readable type and a clear focal action
  accepted as sufficient while palette, typography, imagery, composition,
  tone, or finish still makes the product undesirable or unshareable.
- **Learning theatre:** correct answers immediately after play, vocabulary
  recall, or debrief agreement without novel-case or delayed transfer; beating
  no intervention while failing to beat a cheaper prose or facilitated case.
- **Engagement without agency:** session length, completion, replay, or survival
  optimized by friction, confusion, dominant choices, or score chasing.
- **Reach without influence:** impressions, starts, shares, signatures, ratings,
  or community size presented as qualified learning, institutional reuse, treaty
  adoption, or survival impact.
- **Fixture laundering:** branch frequencies, thresholds, stress worlds, or
  authored outcomes presented as forecasts or evidence that one package works.
- **Advocacy shortcut:** treating pro-treaty sentiment as adequate reasoning, or
  suppressing valid concerns about capture, sovereignty, rights, escalation,
  substitution, false assurance, and safe exit.
- **Complexity leakage:** preserving nuance by putting the atlas, caveats, or a
  dashboard on the phone instead of choosing what the player needs now.
- **Content success mistaken for product success:** expert approval of a claim
  does not show that it is readable, fun, understood, transferable, or useful.
- **A successful run as treaty proof:** winning a diagnostic world does not show
  that a real treaty is feasible, sufficient, or likely.

## Current known state and major uncertainty

The project has a typed opening slice, an intended symbolic model, draft expert
synthesis, and a serious game-design portfolio. It has structural evidence that
the central distinctions can be represented and technical evidence that the
current prototype renders and operates. It does **not** yet have evidence of
fun, comfortable mobile reading, visual appeal, cold-use comprehension,
transfer, demand, sharing, target-audience fit, institutional reuse, or policy
effect. Historical July records describe a rejected mobile spike and integrated
candidate, but their named branches and commits are no longer available in the
current local repository or its remote refs. Jörn's 11 July product feedback
records that the failure included density and type size **and** palette,
typography, portraits/images, composition, and perceived production value; the
unavailable artifacts cannot now be independently inspected from those refs.

The retired GPT-5.5 product at `0c5262c34c423cc62b68124d30d002b4886b879f`
is a reference because Jörn preferred its mobile simplicity and overall UI/UX,
not an approved design to restore wholesale. Its portraits and other visual
choices still require fresh criticism; the old architecture remains quarry,
not authority.

The largest open questions are:

- which compact interaction produces both felt agency and causal transfer;
- how much institutional and scientific detail survives mobile editing without
  producing a false lesson;
- whether retrospectives clarify causality without leaking omniscience into the
  Director's view;
- whether one product can serve play, scrutiny, and facilitated use;
- which premise-qualified audiences will voluntarily play or reuse it;
- whether voluntary mobile play, an explorable reasoning instrument, and a
  facilitated professional case are one product or distinct use contexts;
- whether any observed reasoning change persists and reaches policy-relevant
  contexts; and
- whether reviewed content can be produced and maintained at campaign scale.

## Evidence gates

Gates limit the scope of the next claim; they do not accumulate into automatic
product approval.

| Gate | Evidence needed to advance | What passing does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Coherent concept** | The premise, causal model, claims, player authority, and intended lesson survive mission/model and claim/epistemic challenge; Jörn approves expert-grounded content promoted toward players. | Readability, fun, learning, demand, or feasibility of the full product. |
| **Reviewable prototype** | A rendered, instrumentable artifact on representative desktop and mobile surfaces; code/model boundaries and major interaction states are inspectable. | Public usability. The current spike reached much of this gate while failing the next one. |
| **Human-usable slice** | Fresh target humans on real devices can read, enter, act, understand the immediate feedback, and find the visual presentation credible enough to continue in silent first use; relevant disabled users can complete the path. | Enjoyment, transfer, retention, broad accessibility, or shareability from a small formative sample. |
| **Promising game loop** | Target humans report and demonstrate agency, fair uncertainty, emotional engagement, a satisfying stop, and voluntary interest in continuing; recurring failure modes are understood. | Learning transfer, demand, or campaign-scale durability. |
| **Learning and transfer candidate** | Unprompted causal explanation, differently worded novel-case reasoning, misconception checks, appropriate uncertainty, and delayed evidence improve without material false beliefs across relevant cohorts and against a credible cheaper alternative. | Public-discourse change, policy influence, or real-world treaty adequacy. |
| **Distribution candidate** | Qualified acquisition, recipient engagement, repeat use, and facilitated/institutional reuse are observed with channel, role, and use-context differences preserved. A voluntary game, explainer, and facilitated case may receive different verdicts. | Treaty adoption, operational influence, market size, or virality. Facilitator compliance does not establish voluntary demand. |
| **Release candidate** | Cross-lane evidence holds in an integrated build; content approval, accessibility, performance, privacy, observability, rollback, and maintenance ownership are adequate. | Final product success or durable impact. |
| **Released and maintained product** | Field evidence continues to support readability, play, transfer, distribution, and claim integrity; regressions and stale content are detected and repaired. | Survival impact. Long-range contribution remains an explicitly bounded inference unless stronger external evidence becomes possible. |

Agent review, automated checks, emulation, and expert judgment can reject many
bad artifacts. Only real target humans can establish cold-use readability,
felt agency, emotional response, comprehension, transfer, willingness to
continue or share, and actual reuse. Only real institutional actors and field
observation can establish distribution fit or policy-context use.

## When to reopen this model

Revisit the success model when evidence conflicts with a criterion; a target
audience or use context changes; a product-shape pivot changes what “cold use”
or “distribution” means; players repeatedly learn an unintended lesson; a new
domain judgment reverses a central mechanism; accessibility or mobile needs
cannot coexist with the proposed loop; proxy metrics improve while transfer or
reuse does not; content operations cannot maintain claim status; or a criterion
ceases to affect a real decision.

When artifact evidence conflicts with this document, a reviewer should identify
the conflicting observation, its population and conditions, the status of the
assumption it challenges, and the downstream decision that changes. Observed
behavior outranks a product hypothesis about behavior. Authoritative sources
and Jörn's reviewed judgment outrank a prototype's implied domain claim. One
artifact or cohort narrows a claim; it does not automatically generalize.

The appropriate response may be to reject the artifact, revise the criterion,
split an audience, change product shape, or weaken the impact theory. “The model
says this should work” is never a reason to discount contradictory evidence.
Use [review routing](../review/REVIEW_ROUTING.md) to keep that challenge scoped.

## Verdict discipline

Use verdicts that name the artifact, version, lane, population or viewport,
evidence, and unresolved conditions. Recommended form:

> Accepted for **[lane]** at **[artifact/version]** under **[tested conditions]**.
> This does not approve **[unreviewed lanes, claims, audiences, release, or
> product impact]**.

“Technically valid,” “claim-approved,” “readable in one viewport,” “promising in
formative play,” and “release-ready” are different verdicts. A narrow acceptance
must not be summarized as “the product works.” Deployment, a successful run, or
one favorable review is not product approval. Cross-product approval requires
an explicit synthesis across the relevant lanes and human evidence gates; Jörn
retains domain approval. A product/shape review may favor the next bounded
experiment; it cannot freeze a durable shape without repeated cross-lane human
and use-context evidence.
