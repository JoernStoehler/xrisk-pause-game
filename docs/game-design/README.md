# Game-design portfolio: from opening assay to a playable institution

Status: **design exploration and playability transforms; not an approved product
direction, calibrated game distribution, or approved player-facing expert
content**. Jörn owns domain approval. All proposed dialogue and event details in
this directory remain draft.

## Fixed frame

This exploration assumes the following product premises rather than spending
play time debating them:

- target players grant that superintelligence is possible and is lethal if it
  is not developed safely;
- Jörn's expert judgment is that without an adequate pause we are almost
  certainly dead;
- the United States and China founded a ban treaty for that reason;
- the player is ISIA's Director-General and wants the treaty to succeed; and
- the uncertainty the game explores is how to design and operate an adequate
  pause under different threat models, not a neutral race-versus-pause balance.

That expert judgment is a premise for this design direction, not a displayed
frequency. The game should not encode it as survival odds, a countdown, a
race-versus-pause meter, or diagnostic-world branch frequencies.

The game should give the Director real leverage. Domestic authority still
matters, but "the Director can do nothing until every sovereign actor agrees"
is neither a good first approximation nor a promising source of play. The KISS
default is a small number of potent operating powers whose limits become
concrete through events: joint custody, site monitors, challenge requests,
emergency interruption, controlled model access, and institution-building.

## Design conclusion

Build a **case-driven Director campaign** rather than either a pure swipe-card
deck or an institutional paperwork simulator. Each short chapter should contain
one legible operating problem:

```text
warning or opportunity
        ↓
ask people / inspect the case / prepare an operation
        ↓
issue one consequential order
        ↓
authority and implementation receipts arrive
        ↓
the world visibly changes; uncertainty remains
        ↓
later callback or crisis tests what was actually built
        ↓
optional same-world counterfactual after the chapter
```

The recommended portfolio is not one mechanic repeated forever:

1. **Cabinet drama is the rhythm.** Recurring advisors bring partial views,
   remember choices, develop trust or friction, and make crises personally
   legible. Relationship state changes access and candor, not objective truth.
2. **Operations are the proof.** A compact site/control surface shows credentials
   split, monitors installed, evidence routes opened, exemptions left exposed,
   and response time changed. The reward is visible competence and a changed
   world, not an abstract `+8 enforcement` animation.
3. **Case mysteries are the pressure.** A small number of reports, rival
   hypotheses, deadlines, and reversible/irreversible actions turn the
   observation model into judgment under uncertainty.
4. **Institution-building is preparation.** Between cases, choose a few durable
   capabilities—source protection, emergency legal support, bilingual inspection
   teams, independent technical replication, continuity protocols—that unlock
   options in later cases.
5. **Counterfactual replay is the teaching payoff.** After a resolved chapter,
   optionally replay the same hidden world under another order and show the
   first causal divergence. This is a separate retrospective surface, never
   information the Director had at the time.

The current advisor card remains useful as one interaction inside that loop.
It should not be mistaken for the entire game.

## What makes this fun

The strongest candidate pleasures reinforce, rather than compete with, the
public-understanding goal:

| Pleasure | Minimal mechanic | What it teaches | Primary hazard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Competence under pressure | a deadline, two or three live actions, later receipt | a pause is active crisis management | fake urgency if deadlines are decorative |
| Consequential relationships | recurring advisors whose candor/access responds to treatment | information reaches the DG through people and institutions | friendship becoming a truth bonus |
| Visible world change | sites, access paths, inspection coverage, and continuity visibly change | law, implementation, and factual control diverge | turning the world into a complete dashboard |
| Mystery and inference | sparse evidence, source dependencies, rival hypotheses | alert, violation, report, and belief differ | arbitrary gotchas without retrospective explanation |
| Strategic preparation | a few durable capabilities and held-back emergency powers | institutions create future option value | generic upgrade tree or dominant build order |
| Rescue and recovery | interrupt a run, restore access, protect a source, rebuild control | breach is not automatically terminal | forgiving every failure until choices feel weightless |
| Mastery by replay | same world, different order, first divergence highlighted | intervention-conditional reasoning | implying a unique historical counterfactual |
| Discovery | persistent hidden worlds with correlated mechanisms | uncertainty is structured, not independent punishment | hidden branches reading as rigged RNG |
| Authorship and sharing | a short after-action headline naming one order and consequence | a run is a causal story, not a score | viral fatalism or unapproved factual claims |

Advisor attachment should arise from recognizable professional commitments and
memory: a control commissioner who keeps asking who can physically interrupt a
run; a legal chief who distinguishes fast voluntary access from slower coercive
authority; a source handler who values protection of evidence channels; a
technical critic who is rewarded for correct rejection. None should serve as a
morality-colored answer key.

## Five loop families under stress

Ratings are design judgments for choosing prototypes, not measured player
evidence. `High distortion risk` means the family strongly tempts the model to
teach a shortcut unless constrained.

| Loop family | Core play | Teaching fidelity | Mobile simplicity | Replay | Share/virality | Model-distortion risk | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Cabinet case drama** | browse a few advisor views, confirm an order, receive callbacks | High if every order produces authority and implementation receipts | High: one case, one focal decision | Medium–high through advisor/case variation | High: character plus consequential choice | Medium: advisors can become direct truth sensors | **Core rhythm** |
| **B. Operations control board** | allocate or connect a few real control edges, then test them in an event | Very high for law/control/access distinctions | Medium if limited to one site and 3–5 edges | High across hidden bypass and latency worlds | Medium: satisfying before/after state | Medium: puzzle can imply a perfect solution exists | **Core proof layer, used sparingly** |
| **C. Verification casefile** | inspect evidence, choose next information/action, act before a deadline | Very high for truth/evidence/report distinctions | Medium–high with a short evidence stack | High through correlated hidden causes | High when the reveal is fair | High: mystery conventions reward one correct hidden answer | **Recurring crisis type** |
| **D. Institutional builder** | select a small operating capability, staffing norm, or continuity protocol between cases | High for option value and differentiated institutions | High if choices are rare and concrete | High through portfolios and later callbacks | Medium: institution identity is shareable | High: generic upgrades can become competence/safety meters | **Meta-layer, not the moment-to-moment loop** |
| **E. Counterfactual laboratory** | rerun or compare the same case and reveal first causal divergence | Very high for conditional transfer and model limits | High as an optional after-action timeline | Very high | Medium–high for paired outcome cards | Medium: can imply certainty about real history | **Retrospective/debrief, not the campaign fiction** |

### Why no single family is sufficient

- Cabinet drama without operations becomes opinion shopping.
- Operations without characters becomes infrastructure homework.
- Mysteries without preparation make the player guess at authored twists.
- Institution-building without cases becomes a generic optimization tree.
- Counterfactuals without a first-person run become an educational puzzle set
  with weak emotional stakes.

The smallest promising vertical is therefore **A + one small B interaction +
E**, with C introduced in the next case and D after two cases have demonstrated
why preparation matters.

## Candidate chapter cadence

A chapter should target a satisfying checkpoint after roughly 8–12 decisions,
but this is a user-test hypothesis, not a prescribed session length.

1. **Brief:** one sentence establishes the operational problem and deadline.
2. **Investigate:** the player opens at most three advisor/evidence items in any
   order. Each adds provenance or a rival hypothesis, not lore.
3. **Prepare:** optionally spend or commit one previously built capability.
4. **Order:** choose one concrete action with a foreseeable-effect hint.
5. **Receipts:** show `authorized`, `narrowed`, `delayed`, `implemented`, or
   `blocked`, with named actor and reason.
6. **World beat:** the map/timeline changes; a human callback shows who adapted.
7. **Test:** a later event probes the control or information channel that now
   exists.
8. **After-action:** distinguish what the DG knew, what later became known, and
   what is revealed only for this sampled world.
9. **Compare or continue:** offer same-world replay without forcing it before
   campaign progress.

Not every card needs all nine beats. The cadence belongs to the chapter; a card
is simply one compact beat.

## Transfer targets and tests

The desired outcome is not recall of treaty vocabulary. After play, a player
should be better able to diagnose a new claim.

| Transfer target | In-play contrast | Post-chapter check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "A ban makes the workload illegal, therefore it cannot run." | legal order deters an ordinary actor but leaves a residual startup path; custody closes that path in a matched case | ask which layer changed conduct and which changed feasibility |
| "Monitoring tells us whether cheating occurred." | violation without alert; false alert without violation; report filtered before DG receipt | ask what is known after a sensor anomaly and what action could add evidence |
| "The US/China agreed, so the countries will implement it." | top authorization followed by named domestic gates and parallel PRC civilian/military chains | ask who can authorize, implement, access, and interrupt at one named site |
| "A breach means the treaty failed and everyone dies." | leak, run start, interruption, restoration, takeover, and extinction remain separate | ask which recovery options remain after each transition |
| "Safety progress eventually fills the win bar." | candidate, threat model, evidence, evaluator ecology, authorization, and faithful build diverge | ask what a committee's approval establishes and what it does not |

Use one-sentence novel scenarios rather than repeating the exact game wording.
For example: "A court upholds the ban, but a military site denies the inspection
team entry. What changed?" A good answer distinguishes law from access rather
than remembering a character name.

## Replay and sharing

Two replay modes do different jobs:

- **Same world, different order:** preserves persistent hidden hypotheses and
  stable unrelated draws; highlights the first divergence and downstream path.
- **New world:** tests whether the player's strategy is robust when evasion,
  research transfer, evidence reliability, and implementation differ.

Share output should name a consequential act and a visible consequence:
"I split the launch credentials; three months later the overnight startup test
failed closed." It should not advertise a survival score, a treaty-health score,
an alleged probability, or a frequency drawn from diagnostic fixtures. Shared
copy remains player-facing draft until approved.

## Ideas rejected or deferred

### Four balancing meters as the core game

The retired build's political/intelligence/safety/algorithmic resources created
a fast readable shell, but terminal high/low resource deaths taught the wrong
ontology. They made political legitimacy, evidence, safety adequacy, and
technical progress commensurable and implied that any extreme itself caused
loss. Retain fast decisions, portraits, resumable runs, deterministic history,
and shareable stories; reject generic resource-bar causality.

### Independent random-card survival

A visible hazard deck can efficiently author variety, but arbitrary weighted
cards are unsuitable as the causal foundation. Hidden events, deadlines, and
adaptation must mature whether or not their card happens to be drawn. Otherwise
the pause appears to fail because the deck decided to punish the player.

### Paperwork realism

Requiring the player to draft clauses, fill forms, or manually route every
approval confuses institutional detail with agency. Compress procedure into
named gates and receipts. Expand only the gate that changes a choice or explains
an outcome.

### Grand-strategy map or geopolitical war game

Country-level budgets, armies, and diplomacy would add unsupported priors and
invite the player to treat the pause as a power-balancing sandbox. Activate a
country or crisis when it contains compute, a supply-chain dependency, evidence,
authority, or escalation leverage relevant to a concrete case.

### Generic research and institution tech trees

"Safety +10%" or "inspectorate level 3" would turn unknown adequacy and
task-specific competence into fungible progress. Build named capabilities with
case-specific affordances and failure modes instead.

### Hidden optimal policy and gotcha endings

The expert model does not supply a single known policy for every latent world.
Unavoidable uncertainty can produce loss after a defensible decision, but the
retrospective must show the causal route, evidence limits, and any robust
alternative. Surprise alone is not depth.

### Debate cards that reopen the premise

Do not spend play on a lab CEO arguing that superintelligence cannot be lethal,
on the Director considering whether survival matters, or on a nominally neutral
choice between racing and pausing. Opposition should target whether a proposed
pause design is adequate: scope, evidence, authority, cost, sovereignty,
capture, escalation, and feasible implementation.

## Product cruxes for Jörn review

These are unresolved decisions where domain or product judgment can change the
prototype order:

1. **Direct-power approximation:** which 3–5 operating powers may the DG wield
   directly in the simple public game, and which must always pass through a
   domestic gate?
2. **Core pleasure:** should recurring advisor relationships be a central
   retention mechanism, or should the game stay closer to an impersonal
   institutional case laboratory?
3. **Visible operations:** is a small site/control diagram an acceptable
   approximation, or does it suggest a completeness the real regime cannot
   have?
4. **Preparation budget:** which concrete capabilities are important and
   reviewable enough to become the first four institution-building choices?
5. **Failure pressure:** how often should a defensible order fail in a sampled
   campaign before the experience becomes fatalistic rather than instructive?
   No percentage should be inferred from current fixtures.
6. **Retrospective truth:** which hidden causes may be revealed after a chapter,
   and which should remain unresolved even then?
7. **Campaign horizon:** should the first public arc cover the opening year,
   several crisis chapters across years, or one tightly authored opening case?
8. **Character canon:** should advisors be stable fictional individuals, stable
   offices with changing holders, or a mixture?

## Prototype gate

Before building a long campaign, implement or paper-test one case with the three
grammars in [control-crux-event-grammars.md](control-crux-event-grammars.md).
Advance only if pause-aware but treaty-naive players can:

- identify the concrete order they issued without rereading it;
- explain at least two distinct layers between agreement and stopped hardware;
- predict why a matched order can deter one actor yet fail to block another;
- distinguish a receipt from ground truth;
- say what they would test in a same-world replay; and
- report at least one source of pleasure other than "I learned something."

This is a design gate, not a claim that any particular sample size validates the
product. The initial UX plan's small silent tests should be used to find failure
modes before broader measurement.
