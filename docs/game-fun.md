## The pleasure worth testing

The project needs players to *want* to continue making difficult, intelligible decisions while discovering that a pause demands ongoing work. The old `docs/game-design/README.md`, “What makes this fun” and “Five loop families under stress,” proposed several pleasures: acting competently under pressure, finding out what sparse evidence means, seeing an intervention change the world, preparing an institution for later crises, recovering after a breach, and comparing consequences of different actions. These are **project design hypotheses**, not observations about this audience. They are useful because each could make an institutional distinction felt through action. None requires the old campaign, advisor cast, cards, color choices, or authored case structure.

The most promising *general* mechanism is bounded leverage: the player sees a consequential uncertainty, chooses an action they can plausibly take, receives a legible account of who accepted or blocked it, and later discovers what changed. A good surprise should expose a causal dependence that could have been reasoned about, while preserving uncertainty the player could not have known. A bad surprise feels like a concealed answer key. The agency test is whether two plausible actions change a meaningful future option or outcome for reasons a player can explain—not whether a screen registers a choice. This is an inference from `docs/product/SUCCESS_MODEL.md`, “Game feel and agency” and “Failure modes and proxy traps,” plus the anecdotal arbitrary-outcome warning in `docs/research/product-ux-research-2026-07-11.md`, “What existing product evidence establishes.”

## Rival forms and failure conditions

| Candidate source of pleasure | Why it might fit | What would count against it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Operational puzzle:** alter a few concrete access, custody, observation, or response paths, then see a stress event test them. | Makes control visible without reducing it to a treaty-health meter. | People read it as infrastructure homework, or a diagram implies every relevant route is known and closable. |
| **Evidence mystery:** investigate reports, source dependencies, and rival explanations before a time-sensitive action. | Makes truth, trace, belief, and finding separable. | A reveal rewards guessing the author's twist; the evidence could not support proportionate action. |
| **Institutional drama:** negotiate with people whose commitments, trust, and authority affect what can be done. | Makes coordination and legitimacy emotionally concrete. | A recurring character becomes a reliable truth oracle, or relationships replace mechanisms with popularity management. |
| **Strategic preparation and recovery:** invest in capabilities that create later options; repair damaged control after a setback. | Makes a pause active crisis management with nonterminal failures. | A dominant upgrade order appears, or every failure is easily undone and choices lose weight. |
| **Short comparative explorable:** inspect and manipulate two trajectories of one situation. | May deliver a discovery and mastery loop at phone scale. | Users treat it as a lesson or quiz rather than play; a cheaper static explanation teaches as well. |

The older `docs/product/PRODUCT_ARCHITECTURE_PORTFOLIO.md`, “Shape portfolio,” keeps integrated campaign, separate products, and layered modes live. Its judgments about which form might favor engagement are **agent synthesis**; no form has won a human comparison. A facilitated role-play or tabletop case is also live if social negotiation supplies the pleasure and agency that a solo mobile artifact cannot. That would require distinguishing enjoyment created by the facilitator and group from enjoyment created by the artifact.

The first discriminating artifact can be very small: one real decision with at least two serious actions, an implementation result, a later consequence, and a visible path from action to result. Preserve the same domain mechanism across materially different presentations so player response is about interaction rather than different facts. Observe silent first use on actual phones, then ask where participants felt leverage, surprise, frustration, curiosity, and a satisfying stop; inspect what they *do* as well as what they say. A voluntary second case is a signal, while completion time, replay count, and agent approval alone cannot establish fun. This test logic comes from `docs/product/PRIORITY_DECISION_LEDGER.md`, “Current allocation” and “Strongest live alternatives,” and `docs/review/REVIEW_ROUTING.md`, “Game feel and agency.”

The view should change if a static or facilitated form produces similar agency and understanding at much lower cost; if the game-like interaction creates false confidence in a complete control map; or if a visually credible prototype still feels like paperwork. Conversely, repeated player accounts of consequential, fair choices and voluntary continuation across cohorts would justify testing continuity, emotional attachment, and longer forms. Current coverage has no human play evidence for any of these mechanisms and no reliable session-length target; the July product/UX scan is a dated analogue scan, not current market validation.
