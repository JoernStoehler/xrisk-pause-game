## What the player should be able to distinguish

The most distinctive teaching candidate is the work *between* agreeing to pause and actually controlling dangerous activity. A rule may create authority without access; access may permit inspection without custody; a sensor may yield a trace without a reliable interpretation; a finding may authorize a response that arrives too late; interruption may still leave recovery, substitution, or escalation problems. A pause therefore needs active crisis management, not just a signature. This is the auditor's **project inference** from a purposive English-language discourse sample, not a population estimate: `docs/research/public-discourse-audit-2026-07-11.md`, “Answer first,” “Question and method,” and “Sampling and evidence limitations.” `docs/review/PUBLIC_DISCOURSE_PRIORITIES.md`, “Tiered targets,” ranks the operational middle as a prototype target; that ranking is likewise an ordinal team judgment.

Other useful distinctions depend on the threat model: unilateral restraint, domestic prohibition, reciprocal treaty, and voluntary threshold are different policies; imperfect verification can help in one world and fail in another; compute control can be a useful chain or the wrong surface; treaty operation can worsen capture, rights, sovereignty, or escalation risks even when it improves compliance. Safe exit requires evidence, recognition, authorization, and implementation rather than a generic research-progress bar. These are candidate learning targets from `docs/review/PUBLIC_DISCOURSE_PRIORITIES.md`, “Tiered targets”; their relative causal weights and some AI-specific implications remain unresolved. A scenario frequency, threshold, or winning package must not be taught as a forecast or universally adequate solution.

## False lessons to watch for

Teaching through action can make a simplified institution seem more omnipotent than prose would. Watch especially for players inferring that law physically stops a workload; that a Director's order is automatically implemented; that no alert means no violation; that any alert proves cheating; that a successful diagnostic run establishes treaty feasibility; that stronger monitoring is always better; or that a breach makes recovery impossible. The old `docs/game-design/README.md`, “Transfer targets and tests,” supplies examples, but its specific Director campaign is a conditional design hypothesis. The source audit's strongest skeptical concerns—capture, inequity, surveillance, substitution, conflict, and foregone benefits—must remain live possibilities (`docs/research/public-discourse-audit-2026-07-11.md`, “Answer first” and “Conflicts and claim status”). A player who recites the layer names yet confidently dismisses a competent objection has not achieved the intended judgment.

## Evidence that would discriminate

Choose one named distinction and one likely false inference per small artifact. Before debriefing, ask for an unprompted explanation of an outcome; then present a differently worded, unfamiliar case with a tempting shortcut and a serious objection. For example, if a court upholds a ban but a protected site denies access, ask what the ruling changed, what remains unknown, and which actions could alter factual control. Score causal separation and calibrated uncertainty, not vocabulary recall or selection of the authored “correct” action. Repeat with a delayed novel case to test persistence and flexible use. This approach is proposed in `docs/review/REVIEW_ROUTING.md`, “Comprehension, learning, and transfer,” and `docs/product/COMPACT_LOOP_EXPERIMENT.md`, “Novel transfer probe”; it has not been run on target humans.

Compare against a credible cheaper explanation or facilitated case containing the same substantive facts. Also separate voluntary use from workshop use, because an instructor may cause the learning. An immediate improvement on the played case would justify the next test, not a transfer claim. Novel-case performance without false-belief gains, some persistence after delay, and performance beyond the comparator would be stronger evidence for the interactive route. Evidence that the comparator matches it, or that play increases confidence in an inadequate package, would favor a simpler teaching form or a different mechanic. The stronger interpretation would still be limited to the tested distinction, audience, context, and delay (`docs/product/SUCCESS_MODEL.md`, “Evidence gates”).

The July discourse audit and review priorities identify possible teaching gaps, not which errors actual players hold or how common they are. A future corpus refresh and participant interviews should test which misunderstandings and serious objections matter to the intended audiences before broad content production.
