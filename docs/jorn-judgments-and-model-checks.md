## Recorded qualitative checks

A credible model must distinguish the existence of danger from effective intervention: “risk, therefore do anything” is inadequate because many actions have no effect, some are harmful, few help, and fewer suffice. Conversely, difficulty of enforcement is an implementation challenge, not evidence that a pause is impossible or inferior (May 11, numbered response 1; response 5). The pause described is global, with shared extinction avoidance interests, and requires concrete monitoring and enforcement mechanisms. Their omissions should create realistic warning and response delays (May 11, Treaty Feasibility Follow-Up; see also response 5).

In the direct May record, Jörn guesses that a treaty may need at least roughly 20 years, with large uncertainty (shorter if lucky, longer if unlucky); early actions may be similar whether it lasts 5, 20, or 50 years. He is unenthusiastic about six-month pauses and about 20 years of inactivity followed by ending restrictions in the same unsafe position. This is a conditional horizon and active-work judgment, not a fixed game duration ([May responses 11 and 15](../interviews/jorn.md#2026-05-11-responses-to-fake-public-discourse-quotes)).

A measure can have little direct effect on survival yet matter by enabling later effective controls. Jörn specifically distinguishes weak audits, licensing, or evaluations in isolation from their possible causal role in obtaining stronger action. Check both immediate restraint and the path to subsequent sufficient intervention; his qualitative language is not a calibrated forecast ([May Sufficiency / Fake Hope](../interviews/jorn.md#2026-05-11-sufficiency--fake-hope)).

Capability research is not a single “AI helps research” edge. Jörn separates labor (idea generation, coding, experiment selection), models as test subjects for interpretability/training research, and work made possible by model inference or internals beyond realistic human labor (May 11, Capability Research / Dual-Use Safety Work). Open model danger depends on use and accumulated research, which is hard to predict in advance; a useful model check is whether research controls can express uncertainty and learning costs, not whether each small group is assumed dangerous. Jörn explicitly rejects translating sampled game runs into claims about the one real-world trajectory (May 11, Research Controls Under Uncertainty).

Freedom restrictions and safety research require careful distinctions. A treaty can restrict resource use and sharing while leaving private thought unrestricted; the relevant cost-benefit argument is a specific compute/research tradeoff, not generic “freedom versus safety.” Jörn argues against bundling unrelated political issues into the treaty because changing coalitions could destabilize it (May 11, Research Controls Under Uncertainty). “Safety research” is not automatically benign: it can have capability spillovers, and the boundary may be uncertain. Preserve the option for selective restrictions and the unresolved judgment about where to place them rather than assuming either all research is safe or every restriction is justified.

The July control record endorses separate improvement carriers and fizzle points: context-local procedure, communicable theory, scaffold persistence and trained successors do not automatically transfer into one another. It also distinguishes deterrence from proactive physical control. Penalties can deter ordinary actors; fast decisive runs, expected impunity or completion before enforcement need custody and interruption. Controls reduce feasible routes and raise cost/exposure; they need not make evasion impossible. Jörn’s technically preferred controls could reach old consumer GPUs, while he judges this politically unavailable (July 11, “Reinvestment,” “Punishment and proactive control,” and “Degrees of prevention and political willingness”).

Treat breach, recovery and terminal loss as separate states. Leakage can raise future danger; a run can be interrupted; a signatory can withdraw and re-enter. None alone means extinction. Whether response beats hidden internal improvement depends on time and route (July 11, “Breaches are hazard transitions, not terminal outcomes”). For teaching, collapsing these into a treaty-health bar erases consequential response choices.

The safety route is especially uncertain: none of the concrete routes Jörn had brainstormed looked adequate; his leading speculative bet involved uploaded and intelligence-augmented humans in an institution avoiding important ordinary human errors. This is not an empirical forecast or equal weighting of routes. Adequacy, human recognition, authorization and faithful implementation are distinct. Prestige does not establish the skill of rejecting attractive but failing proposals. The response’s publication/secrecy discussion is expressly incomplete (July 11, “Current route judgment,” “Institutional adequacy,” and “Research secrecy and legibility”).
