## What a treaty can cause

A treaty can define prohibited and permitted conduct, require declarations and access, create a joint body and procedures, and make reciprocal restraint legible. It cannot itself enter a private site, control a scheduler, discover every covert cluster, or compel a state that has lost operational control. For each proposed intervention, trace **agreement → domestic authority and funds → implementer → physical access and control → evidence → interpretation → response**. This is the project's structural inference from the archived `docs/game-model/actor-authority-opening.md` and `docs/game-model/causal-atlas.md` at `2552820`, checkable with `git show 2552820:<path>`. The July US and PRC authority maps there are dated research snapshots, not present-law guarantees.

The annotated [treaty proposal](../literature/iabied-treaty.md) is a concrete candidate design, especially Articles III–X and XII–XV. Its thresholds, 120-day consolidation deadline, institutional votes, and withdrawal terms are *authors' choices*, not a settled minimum treaty. [Compute governance](../literature/sastry-heim-compute-governance-2024.md), especially “Why Compute Governance Is Attractive,” “Visibility,” “Enforcement,” and “Risks,” explains why concentrated compute supply can be governed while documenting limitations and costs. The operating package depends on which dangerous capability routes remain feasible below, outside, or around a compute perimeter.

The proposal’s Articles XIV–XV concern amendment and withdrawal/duration; they do not establish a safety-evidence standard for an ASI build. Article IV provides limited monitored research exceptions, which are different from the archived project’s safety-exit question. In the [direct May record, responses 11 and 15](../interviews/jorn.md#2026-05-11-responses-to-fake-public-discourse-quotes), Jörn guesses a pause may need roughly 20 years, potentially shorter if lucky or longer if unlucky, with similar early actions across 5/20/50-year horizons. He is unenthusiastic about six-month pauses and passive waiting followed by an unsafe exit; this is not a prescribed game duration.

A building block can be valuable as an enabling step even if weak in isolation: ask both what it restrains now and whether it causes later effective control. This distinction comes from [Jörn’s May Sufficiency / Fake Hope response](../interviews/jorn.md#2026-05-11-sufficiency--fake-hope), not a quantitative claim about audit or licensing effectiveness.

## Practical use and live alternatives

For a concrete question, first name the threat route and deadline: a declared frontier run, covert state project, third-country reconstruction, low-compute algorithmic breakthrough, misuse of permitted inference, or unsafe authorized successor. Then ask what the treaty can prohibit, what domestic actor can implement it, which assets it actually reaches, what evidence arrives before the route completes, and who can repair a failure. Deterrence and punishment may restrain ordinary actors; direct interruption matters when an actor accepts sanctions or can finish before them. Jörn's July view favors both, as a **polished paraphrase rather than quotation**; see [the preserved July record, “Punishment and proactive control”](../interviews/jorn.md#punishment-and-proactive-control).

Open choices include centralized versus party-led verification; early monitors versus rapid consolidation or destruction; broad inference permission versus restricted access; public research versus compartmentalization; and intrusive cross-inspection versus politically durable managed access. No retained source calibrates their comparative success under an AI takeoff race. Historical treaty analogies suggest mechanisms, not a transferable annual failure rate. A future thread needs current legal checks by jurisdiction, engineering evidence on detection and halt latency, bargaining evidence on access, and Jörn's judgment about the assumed threat before selecting a package.
