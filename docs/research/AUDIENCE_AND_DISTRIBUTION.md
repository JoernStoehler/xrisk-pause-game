# Audience and distribution research

Date checked: 11 July 2026. Status: product-research synthesis, not a market-size
estimate, demand finding, forecast, or Jörn-approved player-facing claim set.
The research target is people who already grant, or are open to granting, that
advanced AI could cause catastrophic or lethal loss of human control. It does
not treat persuading generic AI-risk sceptics as the product's first job.

## Answer first

The most plausible first audience is a set of overlapping, premise-qualified
communities rather than a mass consumer segment: PauseAI and adjacent AI-safety
organisers; EA/rationalist/alignment readers who are willing to argue about
mechanisms; and AI-governance, security, and policy educators who can use a
short facilitated case. These groups are reachable through existing community
channels and can plausibly affect how a pause is designed or operated, but their
size and willingness to adopt this game are unknown.

The game should be distributed as a useful reasoning instrument—paired traces,
postmortems, and a short mobile case—not as an extinction-persuasion advert.
That distinction matters: a PauseAI chapter may provide a workshop and a policy
contact, while a social-video viewer may provide reach but no evidence of treaty
influence. Track these outcomes separately.

The first release should therefore optimise for **qualified learning and
institutional reuse**, with public reach as a measured secondary outcome. A
small number of credible facilitators can matter more for treaty operation than
many unqualified visits; that is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion.

## Comparable artifacts and transferable mechanisms

These are design analogues, not evidence that this product has demand.

| Artifact | Observable product/channel fact | Transferable mechanism | What it does **not** establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [The Evolution of Trust](https://ncase.me/trust/words.html) | A free, open-web explorable explanation advertises a roughly 30-minute play time and teaches repeated-game cooperation through interactive examples. | A concrete puzzle, immediate experimentation, and visible model changes can make institutional abstractions discussable; source and translations lower sharing friction. | No reliable audience size, retention, or treaty-learning effect is reported on the artifact page. |
| [Reigns](https://www.reignsgames.com/) | The official site presents swipe decisions, temporary balance, uncertainty, branching consequences and replay-friendly dynastic progression; it explicitly frames mobile decision-making. | Short consequential choices, delayed consequences and replay can fit mobile attention while preserving uncertainty. The treaty game needs causal receipts so uncertainty does not feel arbitrary. | Commercial popularity or ratings do not identify which mechanic teaches treaty operations; fictional monarchy is not an international agency. |
| [Darfur is Dying sharing study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563213002513) | A panel study examined intentions and actual sharing of a prosocial online game; positive emotion predicted sharing during play and negative emotion predicted sharing after play, while enjoyment did not predict actual sharing. | Put a shareable, emotionally meaningful decision or postmortem after a gameplay beat; instrument actual referrals, not button presses. | 2014 college-student sample and one game do not predict this audience's virality or policy influence. |
| [Speak in My Language](https://jprm.scholasticahq.com/article/161952-speak-in-my-language) | A 2026 case used two low-cost games with a game master and reached 200 community members in five hours, reporting awareness, empathy and advocacy interest. | Facilitated sessions can outperform a link-only launch for discussion and qualified feedback; package a facilitator guide and printable/shareable traces. | Small case study, different topic and no randomized long-term behavior measure. |
| [ICPEM serious games](https://www.theicpem.org/games) | An emergency-management organisation explicitly builds games for professionals, children and the public to develop risk/resilience understanding. | Frame the game as a risk-management practice tool and seek emergency/policy-education partners, not only game storefront traffic. | Organisational intent is not evidence of effectiveness or audience demand. |

## Distribution hypotheses (ranked, falsifiable)

These are experiments, not claims about demand.

1. **Facilitated, premise-qualified workshops beat a link-only launch on
   mechanism learning.** Recruit matched sessions through PauseAI, EA/AI-safety
   and one policy-education partner. Compare a 20–30 minute facilitator-led
   case with an unguided link. Primary measures: correct separation of event,
   trace, belief, legal finding and response; delayed transfer to a novel trace.
   Secondary: completion, replay and qualified referral. Falsifier: no
   comprehension or transfer advantage after controlling for prior familiarity.
2. **A paired-trace/postmortem share card generates more qualified referrals
   than generic risk framing.** Randomise landing pages between (a) a concrete
   same-event/different-evidence contrast and (b) a generic “AI could be
   dangerous” pitch. Record source, prior premise familiarity, start and
   completion; define a qualified referral as a referred player who completes
   the first causal checkpoint. Falsifier: no difference after source and
   familiarity adjustment.
3. **A 5–8 minute mobile opening plus optional deep dive has a safer completion
   funnel than a single 30-minute introduction.** Test on phones with
   premise-qualified recruits and randomise checkpoint placement. Measure quit
   hazard, causal-recall score and voluntary deep-dive starts; do not optimise
   raw time-on-page. Falsifier: the short opening does not improve checkpoint
   completion or harms transfer.
4. **Institutional reuse has lower reach but higher treaty-operation relevance
   than social distribution.** Give policy educators a facilitator kit and give
   social creators a link/snippet kit. Compare number of participants reached
   with the number who can correctly propose a proportionate response to a new
   evidence/authority case and who reuse the artifact in a class or briefing.
   Falsifier: no difference in transfer/reuse, or social distribution matches
   qualified influence at materially lower cost.

## Evidence gaps, counterevidence and guardrails

- No source establishes demand, retention, willingness to pay, or a reachable
  market size for this game. Community pages and self-reported programme
  figures establish channels, not denominator estimates.
- AI-author risk surveys establish a premise-qualified research subset, not a
  coalition for a pause, a view on treaty design, or a prediction of policy
  adoption. Treat all percentages as survey results with their own sampling and
  wording limits.
- Broad regulation support is a weak proxy. Gallup explicitly says its 2025
  question did not ask which rules respondents support. A public may favour
  safety rules while rejecting a global pause, centralised inspection or
  surveillance.
- Serious-game learning evidence is strongest in bounded educational settings;
  transfer to international treaty operation is untested here. The viral-sharing
  study is old and topic-specific. Facilitation may be the causal ingredient,
  not the game alone.
- Premise-qualified communities may have ceiling effects, factional conflict,
  or distrust of advocacy framing. Include technically and politically serious
  objections—capture, sovereignty, false assurance, overhang and escalation—in
  the test cases.
- Reach is not influence. Report at least four separate funnels: exposure,
  qualified start, causal learning/transfer, and institutional reuse or policy
  contact. Never report shares, votes, signatures or page views as treaty
  influence.
- The public-discourse audit on `codex/public-discourse-research` found the
  strongest unmet teaching need to be the operational middle between a rule and
  control: observation, evidence routing, authority, response and repair. This
  research therefore recommends distributing that mechanism laboratory, not a
  generic “AI doom” message. The audit itself is purposive and not a prevalence
  estimate; use the `source/public-discourse-audit-2026-07-11.md` copy on the
  `codex/public-discourse-research` branch for the project record.

## Cheap evidence collection before scaling

1. Run three 6–8-person silent-first-use sessions (PauseAI/AI-safety, policy
   educators, and game/interactive-explanation users). Capture source, prior
   premise familiarity, quit point, causal-recall answers and misconceptions;
   interview only after stopping.
2. Ask five potential facilitators to run the same 20-minute case with a
   one-page guide. Record preparation time, participant questions, and whether
   they request a second use. A request is a signal of utility, not proof of
   demand.
3. Publish two instrumented share cards to existing newsletters or community
   calendars with explicit permission. Use referral codes and a copy-link
   fallback; measure qualified starts, not impressions.
4. Pilot a policy-class or tabletop version and test delayed transfer one week
   later. Any claim about treaty-operation influence remains draft until this
   evidence and domain review exist.
