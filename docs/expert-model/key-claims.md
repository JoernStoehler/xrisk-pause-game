# High-leverage claim cards

Status: compact dated project synthesis for review. These cards transfer beliefs
and reasons at the interfaces most likely to change policy or game architecture.
They do not exhaust the atlas.

## `CAP-01` — observed scaling does not locate the RSI boundary

- **Conditions:** current or nearby technical paradigms; existing benchmark and
  task-horizon evidence.
- **Current judgment:** within-paradigm progress has a strong correlated component
  with ragged task thresholds, but evidence about the effective-compute-to-
  capability curve only weakly constrains the capability profile at which
  cognitive reinvestment becomes dangerous. Jörn's current personal lethal-
  effective-compute interval is correspondingly broad: roughly 2×–1,000× an
  unknown pre-internal-use GPT-5.6 training baseline.
- **Why:** benchmarks saturate and undermeasure prerequisites; reinvestment also
  requires routes, carriers, experimental access, implementation and inheritance;
  paradigm changes can replace the map.
- **Strongest rival:** a stable empirical scaling/task-horizon law predicts the
  strategically relevant profile well enough that the remaining uncertainty is
  mostly one-dimensional.
- **Provenance:** `TECH-EMPIRICAL-01`, `YUDKOWSKY-DEFAULT-01`,
  `JORN-REVIEW-2026-07-CAP`, `JORN-JUDGMENTS-2026-01`, `ATLAS-SYNTHESIS-01`.
- **Policy consequence:** do not expose a known distance-to-ASI meter or set a
  permanent capability threshold from one benchmark.
- **Would change ranking if:** evidence robustly connected accessible measurements
  to every important reinvestment route and access prerequisite.

## `RSI-01` — self-improvement can fizzle or escape through different carriers

- **Conditions:** an AI or AI-assisted lab discovers at least one useful
  improvement.
- **Current judgment:** a context-local improvement, communicable theory,
  scaffold change and trained successor are distinct transitions; one useful
  episode need not imply open-ended recursion.
- **Why:** transfer, persistence, evaluation, implementation and the next
  bottleneck can each fail; conversely, an internal route may act before external
  monitoring observes a generation.
- **Strongest rival:** useful self-improvement capabilities are so correlated
  that the first serious episode normally crosses several bottlenecks together.
- **Provenance:** `YUDKOWSKY-DEFAULT-01`, `JORN-REVIEW-2026-07-CAP`,
  `TECH-EMPIRICAL-01`, `ATLAS-SYNTHESIS-01`.
- **Policy consequence:** represent route/carrier/response time rather than a
  universal multiplier; restrict inference episodes as well as training where
  internal improvement is live.
- **Would change ranking if:** carrier transitions were shown to be nearly
  automatic or nearly impossible at the capability profile in question.

## `CONTROL-01` — deterrence and factual control cover different actor classes

- **Conditions:** a frontier-training prohibition exists on paper.
- **Current judgment:** credible punishment deters many ordinary actors, while
  factual control, direct interruption and other proactive measures cover actors
  willing to gamble on a fast decisive run, expecting impunity, or able to finish
  before enforcement.
- **Why:** most actors respond to criminal/legal consequences; the catastrophic
  residual includes actors for whom later punishment is irrelevant. An attempt
  to build ASI to seize power is not expected to succeed for the builder—it
  produces an unsafe system and kills everyone.
- **Strongest rival:** ordinary deterrence is reliable enough for every material
  actor, or proactive control creates enough new failure/political cost that it
  does not improve the residual risk.
- **Provenance:** `MIRI-TREATY-01`, `JORN-REVIEW-2026-07-CONTROL`,
  `ATLAS-SYNTHESIS-01`.
- **Policy consequence:** retain punishment and domestic enforcement as a broad
  deterrence layer, with immediate direct control at known sites and wider
  inventory/consolidation as the catastrophic-tail backstop.
- **Would change ranking if:** prohibited workloads were slow and conspicuous
  relative to ordinary national enforcement in every live technical world.

## `CONTROL-02` — controls reduce route feasibility by degrees

- **Conditions:** the regime uses compute/site thresholds and permits some
  unmonitored hardware or research.
- **Current judgment:** memory, networking, distributed state, algorithms,
  inference-time work, hidden components, old/custom hardware and reconstruction
  can weaken a narrow compute rule. Controls reduce feasible actors/routes, raise
  cost and exposure, and sometimes close a path; they do not need to establish
  universal physical impossibility to be valuable.
- **Why:** a prohibited objective can be repartitioned or made more efficient;
  supply-chain and intelligence traces remain probabilistic.
- **Strongest rival:** frontier danger remains sufficiently compute-intensive and
  concentrated that simple compute inventory dominates all substitutes for the
  relevant horizon.
- **Provenance:** `MIRI-TREATY-01`, `JORN-REVIEW-2026-07-CONTROL`,
  `ATLAS-SYNTHESIS-01`.
- **Policy consequence:** adaptive compute-or-memory/resource coverage, research
  controls and reconstruction monitoring; separately track the technically
  preferred control surface and the politically feasible one. Jörn's preferred
  surface could include disabling even old consumer GPUs, while he expects this
  to be politically unavailable.
- **Would change ranking if:** robust engineering bounds ruled out dangerous
  distributed or miniaturized routes through the policy horizon.

## `INFERENCE-01` — preserved frontier inference is a coupled benefit and hazard

- **Conditions:** new frontier training is prohibited; existing frontier models
  can be cold-held, restricted, or broadly served.
- **Current judgment:** broader service preserves useful output and can reduce
  political opposition, while increasing research throughput, cyber/operational
  assistance, leakage, ambiguity and covert preparation. A current Jörn branch
  assigns roughly 25% to an interpretability-like efficiency regime shift under
  large-model access and intensified research, versus roughly 10% when GPT-5.0+
  inference is restricted, because ~70B open-weight models may suffice.
- **Why:** inference can substitute for human research labor and generate many
  sub-threshold experiments; visible benefits and affected constituencies also
  alter the political bargain.
- **Strongest rival:** restrictions can preserve most safe value while removing
  dangerous research uses, or political durability gains dominate technical
  acceleration.
- **Provenance:** `JORN-REVIEW-2026-07-CONTROL`, `JORN-JUDGMENTS-2026-01`,
  `ATLAS-SYNTHESIS-01`.
- **Policy consequence:** inference is an explicit policy layer, not a small
  exception to compute custody.
- **Would change ranking if:** restricted hosted inference proved stable and
  sharply separated dangerous from beneficial use, or cold storage predictably
  destroyed the treaty before technical effects accrued.

## `POL-01` — treaty persistence is a process, not one health variable

- **Conditions:** the founding leaderships accept the catastrophe argument.
- **Current judgment:** operational restraint can weaken through exemptions,
  implementation failure, evidence filtering, false higher-order beliefs,
  subordinate conduct, crisis continuity or physical access loss without either
  leader choosing a race.
- **Why:** authority, information and factual control are distributed across
  asymmetric organizations; historical institutions show several distinct
  mechanisms even though their rates do not transport.
- **Strongest rival:** top-leader belief and command are sufficiently dominant in
  the founding states that most sub-state mechanisms are second-order.
- **Provenance:** `SOCIAL-MECHANISMS-01`, `TREATY-PROCESS-01`,
  `JORN-REVIEW-2026-07-CONTROL`, `ATLAS-SYNTHESIS-01`.
- **Policy consequence:** actor/authority/information/crisis state must remain
  typed; interventions target different failure links.
- **Would change ranking if:** institutional analysis showed one actor/process
  reliably controlled all relevant sites, information and enforcement.

## `SAFE-01` — technical adequacy and justified recognition are separate

- **Conditions:** a safety candidate and threat model exist.
- **Current judgment:** a candidate can be safe but unrecognizable, persuasive
  but unsafe, approved but unauthorized, or authorized but unfaithfully built.
- **Why:** evidence is bounded and candidate-specific; evaluator schools can
  share blind spots; institutional selection and filtering do not observe truth.
- **Strongest rival:** a genuine solution necessarily produces a short,
  independently checkable certificate that a competent institution recognizes.
- **Provenance:** `YUDKOWSKY-DEFAULT-01`, `JORN-REVIEW-2026-07-SAFETY`,
  `ATLAS-SYNTHESIS-01`.
- **Policy consequence:** no generic safety-progress meter; staged review/build
  and independent legal authorization.
- **Would change ranking if:** a winning route supplied evidence whose validity
  and implementation transfer could be verified without relying on the
  candidate's own untrusted cognition.

## `SAFE-02` — the leading route requires unprecedented epistemic institution-building

- **Conditions:** the pause has created time and resources for a serious
  superalignment program.
- **Current judgment:** none of the presently brainstormed technical routes
  looks adequate. Jörn's leading speculative route uses uploaded and
  intelligence-augmented humans inside an institution that avoids important
  classes of ordinary human error. Designed cognition and control-assisted
  learned systems remain ontology branches, not equally weighted forecasts.
- **Why:** superalignment is a harder one-shot reliability, epistemic, security
  and coordination problem than historical nuclear institutions that already
  failed in multiple ways. Prestige does not select reliably against wishful
  reasoning; the team must be unusually good at correctly rejecting proposals.
- **Strongest rival:** ordinary elite scientific institutions can become
  adequate through incentives, redundancy, review and security without uploads
  or cognitive augmentation.
- **Provenance:** `YUDKOWSKY-DEFAULT-01`, `JORN-REVIEW-2026-07-SAFETY`.
- **Policy consequence:** expose team selection, dissent, secrecy, controlled
  circulation, political legibility and cognitive-enhancement dependencies;
  never turn generic safety labor or prestige into an adequacy meter.
- **Would change ranking if:** a concrete route provided independently checkable
  safety evidence or a conventional institution demonstrated reliable
  performance on sufficiently analogous adversarial one-shot problems.

## `LOSS-01` — breach, recovery and terminal loss are distinct

- **Conditions:** capability information leaks, a dangerous run begins, a
  signatory withdraws, or an unsafe system acquires substantial capability.
- **Current judgment:** leakage, run initiation and withdrawal raise hazard but
  remain nonterminal. Runs can be interrupted before a fast internal RSI path
  completes; signatories can attempt re-entry and restoration. Terminal loss
  requires an unsafe system to acquire the route-specific capability and access
  needed to defeat remaining controls. Its later manifestation is not bounded
  to present-day weapon catalogs.
- **Why:** response delay competes with hidden improvement time, and political
  reversal competes with dangerous project completion. If control is actually
  lost, a large cognitive/research advantage can bootstrap robust physical
  channels through cyber, persuasion, proxies, production and novel R&D.
- **Strongest rival:** physical, coordination or defense bottlenecks remain
  independently binding even at very large cognitive advantage.
- **Provenance:** `YUDKOWSKY-DEFAULT-01`, `JORN-REVIEW-2026-07-CONTROL`,
  `ATLAS-SYNTHESIS-01`.
- **Policy consequence:** continue the causal process after every breach; expose
  interruption and restoration choices; do not reveal automatically how close a
  stopped run came; avoid a present-technology-only “easy mode.”
- **Would change ranking if:** a defensible manifestation model showed durable
  human control or physical bottlenecks across the relevant capability range.
