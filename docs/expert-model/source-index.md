## Default risk and treaty model

- Eliezer Yudkowsky, [*Intelligence Explosion
  Microeconomics*](https://intelligence.org/files/IEM.pdf): structural questions
  about returns on cognitive reinvestment, local and distributed improvement,
  bottlenecks, speed, copying, hardware, and manifestation. This is not a fitted
  RSI forecast.
- MIRI, [*A Draft of a Treaty, with
  Annotations*](https://ifanyonebuildsit.com/treaty): illustrative prevention
  treaty and provision-by-provision rationale. The authors explicitly label its
  language and numerical thresholds provisional.
- MIRI technical governance, [*An International Agreement to Prevent the
  Premature Creation of Artificial
  Superintelligence*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10783): technical report behind
  the illustrative agreement.
- MIRI TGT, [*Catching illicit distributed training operations during an AI
  pause*](https://techgov.intelligence.org/blog/catching-illicit-distributed-training-operations-during-an-ai-pause): later
  accelerator-memory coverage proposal aimed at making covert distributed
  training more operationally exposed.

## Capability measurement and research production

- METR et al., [*Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long
  Tasks*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499): task-horizon measurement in a
  bounded task ecology; relevant to serial duration and current extrapolation,
  not a universal intelligence law.
- METR, [*RE-Bench*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15114): AI R&D performance across
  time budgets; evidence against a task-independent “copies × ability” research
  production function.
- [*PaperBench*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01848): end-to-end research
  replication and its compositional bottlenecks.
- [*The Darwin Gödel Machine*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954): bounded
  empirical self-improvement through candidate generation, evaluation, and
  selection; evidence for portfolio structure, not an open-ended tail law.
- Besiroglu and Erdil, [*Explosive Growth from AI
  Automation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11690): economic mechanisms and
  complements in automation-driven growth; useful structurally rather than as a
  calibrated local-FOOM model.
- Aghion, Jones, and Jones, [*Artificial Intelligence and Economic
  Growth*](https://web.stanford.edu/~chadj/AJJ-AIandGrowth.pdf): task
  substitution, complementarity, nonrival ideas, and growth regimes.

## Operating verification and institutional process

- IAEA, [*Safeguards explained*](https://www.iaea.org/topics/safeguards-explained):
  the safeguards cycle, state-specific approaches, information evaluation,
  inspection planning, and conclusions.
- IAEA, [*Additional
  Protocol*](https://www.iaea.org/topics/additional-protocol): process response
  to the declared-versus-undeclared activity problem. Useful as an ontology for
  scope, information, and access—not an AI detection rate.
- OPCW, [Chemical Weapons Convention verification and
  inspections](https://www.opcw.org/chemical-weapons-convention): institutional
  precedent for declarations, routine/challenge inspection, confidentiality,
  and compliance processes.

## Political and organizational mechanisms

The atlas draws mechanism vocabulary from work on repeated cooperation,
legalization, rational institutional design, international-organization
delegation, norm contestation, policy feedback, elite cues, blame avoidance,
normal accidents, high-reliability organizations, coercion, and strategic
inspection. These sources should be attached claim-by-claim before public
release. Historical treaty cases supply process traces and rival explanations,
not target-domain annual failure rates.

## Source-priority note

Where a current empirical paper, AI 2040, or another expert disagrees with
Yudkowsky on takeoff or alignment, record the disagreement rather than averaging
the views. Use other authors to add detail where the default model is silent;
do not let greater narrative detail silently outweigh the project's stated
source priority.
