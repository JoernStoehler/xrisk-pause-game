# Technical safety and exit

## The exit problem

The purpose of the pause is not to hold capability fixed forever as an end in itself. It is to avoid an irreversible unsafe build while work proceeds toward some route that can produce a safe successor. The Director-General eventually faces a decision under three different uncertainties:

1. whether a candidate is objectively adequate against a specified threat model;
2. whether available evidence lets competent evaluators recognize that adequacy;
3. whether the institution reports, authorizes, and implements the candidate faithfully.

These must remain separate. A safe but unrecognizable design can leave a healthy institution paused. A false but persuasive case can pass every procedural gate and kill everyone.

## Current prior over winning routes

The route list below is an ontology for preserving distinctions, not a menu of
comparably plausible solutions. Jörn's current judgment is that none of the
concrete routes presently brainstormed looks adequate. His leading bet is that a
successful program involves human uploading and intelligence augmentation, run
by researchers whose enhanced cognition avoids important classes of ordinary
human reasoning and coordination error. This is still a conjecture, not an
available engineering plan.

Any winning route appears to require institution-building beyond historical
precedent. Chernobyl and nuclear non-proliferation were easier reliability and
coordination problems and nevertheless failed in multiple ways. A superalignment
project combines harder epistemics, security, dual-use research, internal
governance and a first-attempt-must-work constraint.

## Candidate route ontology

### Deliberately designed cognition

Researchers develop cognition whose strategically relevant reasoning, objectives, reflection, and successor construction are understood by design rather than grown opaquely through frontier optimization. This could still leave hard problems of value and ontology choice, corrigibility, implementation fidelity, and governance. Its attraction is that the justification for safety need not extrapolate bounded empirical behavior through recursive improvement.

### Uploads or intelligence-enhanced humans

Large amounts of labor still recognizably descended from human cognition perform the theory and design work. A rough expert question may be stated in millions of labor-hours of uploaded or enhanced minds, but the atlas has no justified quantitative answer. This route changes research speed, evaluation, coordination, and governance; it can also leak capabilities or create newly powerful misaligned actors. “Human-derived” is not by itself a safety proof.

### Control-assisted learned system

An opaque learned system performs restricted tasks under monitoring, trusted components, security, anti-collusion measures, and staged access. It helps produce safety work without gaining the means to defeat the process. The main failure is extrapolation: bounded-trial evidence may cease to apply when the system gains situational awareness, extended horizon, new tools, copies, or a route to recursive improvement.

These routes may combine. The atlas does not assert that any is achievable or
recognizable. It records enhanced/uploaded researchers as Jörn's leading
speculative route while retaining the others so the game does not silently rule
out an unknown solution.

## Evidence is candidate- and threat-model-specific

A valid case should identify the candidate, the capabilities it may acquire, the failure modes to be excluded, and the environment in which it operates. Evidence about short-horizon control does not establish reflective alignment. Evidence about one architecture does not automatically transfer to its successor. A safety claim can be true while the institution tests the wrong claim.

Relevant evidence might include construction-level arguments, mechanistic understanding, adversarial tests, formal results, bounded deployment evidence, independent replication, and demonstrated control of the successor process. No checklist is sufficient independent of the route and threat model.

## Evaluator ecology

The Director-General should not receive a noisy direct reading of “true safety.” Evidence passes through:

`candidate behavior and artifacts → evaluator access and methods → school-specific interpretation → dissent and replication → institutional report → legal authorization → implementation`

Evaluator selection is therefore substantive. A competent institution needs multiple relevant schools, access to raw evidence, protection for dissent, adversarial incentives, and the ability to discover that its question set is incomplete. Capture can occur through personnel, scope, security classification, funding, professional consensus, or filtering rather than crude bribery.

Poor institutional health may destroy the signal entirely: advisors can confidently recommend release while lacking the ability to identify the people or arguments capable of evaluating it. Conversely, permanent veto by unanswerable objections can prevent a real solution from being recognized.

Prestige is not a substitute for calibrated pessimism. A key competence is the
ability to identify that an appealing proposal will fail, state why, and then be
vindicated when it fails. Jörn judges the existing ASI-alignment track record of
companies, universities and even highly decorated researchers as negative; past
status therefore does not establish competence under a harder, one-shot
decision. The engine should model correlated wishful error, adversarial dissent
and evaluator selection rather than award correctness from institutional rank.

## Research bans and dual use

The pause must decide which research remains permitted. Foundational deep-learning theory, mathematical work, interpretability, control, efficient training, model construction, and evaluation can contribute to safety while also lowering the compute needed for dangerous capability or revealing a direct route to intelligence construction.

The law may try to distinguish harmful from harmless work by topic, method, compute, access, publication, or intended use. None is clean. A dual-use insight can be privately needed for a safety route yet lethal if published or used on an opaque learned system. Relevant controls include compartmentalization, secure research environments, staged disclosure, controlled experiments, and limits on model assistance. These measures impose epistemic costs by reducing criticism, replication, and cross-field synthesis.

The game need not simulate every research field. It should preserve the dilemma: broader research access can improve the probability of finding a solution and simultaneously accelerate or miniaturize the dangerous build pathway.

A serious program will likely generate foundational results that are themselves
dangerous capability artifacts. Keeping them secret reduces leakage but also
reduces criticism, available human/AI labor, and externally legible evidence of
progress needed for political support. Publication, controlled circulation and
compartmentalization are therefore coupled safety, capability and treaty-
durability decisions. The preferred balance remains under review.

## Exit outcomes

At least four outcomes must be distinguishable:

- objectively adequate, correctly recognized, authorized, and faithfully built;
- objectively adequate but not recognized or not authorized;
- inadequate but institutionally accepted and authorized;
- adequate case and authorization, followed by implementation substitution, sabotage, or error.

The first is the intended victory. The third is a loss even if every formal procedure worked as designed.
