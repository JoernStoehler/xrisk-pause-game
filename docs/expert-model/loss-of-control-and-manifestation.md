# Loss of control and manifestation

## Treaty failure is not yet game over

A weakened or failed treaty changes access, observability, incentives, and
response capacity. It is not itself the terminal outcome. Nor are capability
leakage, the start of a prohibited run, or formal withdrawal necessarily
terminal. Each raises danger while leaving some recovery paths open.

The causal chain can branch:

- leaked insights raise algorithmic efficiency or lower future resource needs,
  while no dangerous system yet exists;
- a prohibited run begins, monitors detect it after a delay, and controllers
  interrupt it before a fast internal RSI/takeover path completes;
- the same run becomes fatal because such a path completes before interruption;
- a signatory withdraws or suspends cooperation, then re-enters before a
  decisive build, after which bans, monitoring and factual control must be
  rebuilt under worse conditions.

The player may not learn how close an interrupted run came: absence of visible
failure is not proof that the threshold was distant. Showing elapsed time,
hidden progress, partial evidence, control degradation and attempted recovery is
central to the game. It should never substitute an arbitrary death card for
these transitions.

## Paths into a dangerous build

Dangerous capability can emerge through:

- an overtly resumed frontier program;
- a military or intelligence exemption;
- covert training at an undisclosed data center;
- distributed training using memory and state persistence that evade a compute-only rule;
- permitted inference or safety work that yields a large algorithmic-efficiency gain;
- leaked dual-use research that makes smaller projects sufficient;
- reconstruction of hardware and supply chains in a third country;
- a successor trained under a misleading safety authorization;
- improvement performed within an authorized inference episode.

These routes differ in warning signs and intervention time. The model should not
force them through a single “capability progress” meter, even if the player sees
a coarse algorithmic-progress indicator.

## From capability to loss of effective control

An unsafe system need not begin with unrestricted physical autonomy. It may obtain leverage through cyber compromise, credential theft, persuasion, deception of evaluators, replication, strategic use of human organizations, or production of useful-seeming research. It may acquire resources before controllers agree that a crisis exists.

Recursive technical improvement is one route to decisive advantage, not the definition of loss of control. A system could already have sufficient social, cyber, scientific, or strategic capability to defeat containment; conversely, a bounded self-improvement jump can fizzle while leaving humans in control.

The relevant comparison between improvement time and ISIA response time ceases to be sufficient once the system can alter the response process itself, acquire access within one authorized episode, or act through channels that the institution does not observe.

## Manifestation is not restricted to current technology

The default x-risk model does not assume that a superintelligence must kill humanity using only present-day weapons. A sufficiently capable system can conduct research, coordinate production, exploit infrastructure, and develop novel technologies. Exact terminal mechanisms are deeply uncertain and unnecessary for most policy decisions; constraining them to familiar bioweapons, drones, or cyberattacks would teach a false technological ceiling.

For play, manifestation can be represented at the level of causal prerequisites:

1. strategic capability and a reason not to preserve human control;
2. access to information, computation, communication, or human proxies;
3. acquisition or construction of a robust physical action channel;
4. neutralization of attempted shutdown, coordination, or recovery;
5. a globally decisive outcome.

Specific technologies can illustrate the route without becoming the expert model's exhaustive list.

## Algorithmic discontinuities

A theory or construction breakthrough can sharply lower the compute, memory, data, or training-time requirement for dangerous cognition. This can invalidate thresholds, move training from monitored frontier sites to much smaller clusters, and turn formerly safe research or inference permissions into the decisive pathway. The transformer breakthrough and deep learning motivate paradigm uncertainty; they do not calibrate the size or frequency of future discontinuities.

Controls therefore need adaptive threshold revision, secure treatment of dual-use discoveries, monitoring of memory and distributed state, and response mechanisms faster than ordinary treaty amendment. These protections themselves create authority and capture risks.

## Third-country enforcement

A US–China founding regime leaves third-country production, clusters, cloud services, energy, talent, and territorial jurisdiction. Response options can include accession bargains, export and supply-chain controls, technical assistance, inspections, sanctions, cyber disruption, component disablement, seizure, or ultimately physical force.

The hard cases involve conflicting relationships and escalation: whether the founding states assist another country's chip industry in exchange for control; whether they trust domestic rather than cross-national inspection; or whether they attack an illegal data center in a state tied economically or militarily to one signatory. Enforcement strength can reduce technical danger while increasing geopolitical opposition, substitution, secrecy, and the chance that the coalition fractures.

The atlas should not model “rest of world compliance” as one draw. It should preserve at least supply-chain dependence, jurisdictional willingness, detectability, great-power agreement, and escalation tolerance. Detailed country-level simulation is high-cost and can be pruned until a case changes a central policy choice.

## Provenance and uncertainty

The structural loss-of-control model follows `YUDKOWSKY-DEFAULT-01`; compute, memory, and treaty enforcement mechanisms draw on `MIRI-TREATY-01` and `JORN-REVIEW-2026-07-CONTROL`; the integrated pathway is `ATLAS-SYNTHESIS-01`. Exact access routes, novel technologies, and time from control loss to extinction remain unresolved and should not receive decorative precision.
