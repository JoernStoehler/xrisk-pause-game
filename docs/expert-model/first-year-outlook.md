# Conditional first-year outlook

## Conditioning world

This outlook covers August 2026 through July 2027 in the game's mainline setup:

- the United States and China are founding signatories;
- ISIA exists and has a Director-General;
- both national leaderships currently accept the need for a frontier-training pause;
- known frontier sites can be approached immediately;
- detailed domestic implementation, reciprocal inspection, inference policy, custody, consolidation, and global accession remain unsettled;
- existing frontier models and inference infrastructure have not necessarily been rolled back.

This is not a forecast that these conditions will occur in reality. It forecasts conditional on them.

This page is a project synthesis (`ATLAS-SYNTHESIS-01`) of the technical, control, and institutional claims in the neighboring pages. Its branch rankings are provisional expert-model judgments rather than results directly estimated from historical data.

## Structurally expected developments

### Known sites can stop before the global regime is complete

National orders, on-site monitors, and direct contact with a small number of known frontier operators can interrupt clearly prohibited large training runs quickly. Universal registration, relocation, component accounting, secure telemetry, and coverage of military or covert systems take longer. The first year therefore probably contains a strong but uneven control surface rather than either “no pause” or complete consolidation.

### The decisive disputes move from the slogan to scope

Early conflict is likely to concern what counts as training, which inference workloads remain allowed, who holds credentials and stop authority, whether military and intelligence clusters are covered, what evidence foreign or ISIA inspectors receive, and how property and compensation are handled. These are not implementation details after the treaty; they determine what treaty physically exists.

### The organization begins with borrowed capacity and distorted information

Instant hiring in the game abstracts away recruitment time, but expertise, clearances, equipment, legal authority, and reliable reporting chains remain uneven. Labs, security agencies, national regulators, and the new inspectorate possess different fragments of relevant knowledge. Evaluator selection and information filtering begin affecting policy before anything recognizable as total institutional capture occurs.

### Permitted inference becomes a coupled political–technical choice

Continuing inference can preserve useful services, economic support, administrative capacity, and safety-research tools. It can also increase experiment throughput, algorithmic progress, cyber capability, persuasive capacity, leakage, and preparation for covert training. The sign and magnitude depend heavily on workload restrictions, model access, logging, memory, tool use, and who controls the service.

## Central branches

The current default is a year of partial implementation and repeated bargaining, not a quiet stable pause and not automatic collapse. Known frontier training is substantially reduced. The residual danger shifts toward exceptions, inference-enabled research, hidden or state-controlled resources, ambiguous workloads, supply-chain adaptation, and institutional inability to distinguish legitimate safety work from capability work.

US–China disagreement is more likely to appear first as access limits, asymmetric implementation, disputed evidence, or exceptions than as an explicit announcement that one side intends to build unsafe superintelligence. Domestic actors within each signatory matter: militaries, intelligence agencies, legislatures, courts, labs, cloud providers, regulators, provinces, and state firms do not share one belief or authority.

Safety research continues, but a justified decision that the alignment problem has been solved within the first year should not be treated as an ordinary milestone. Even a real advance must be connected to a particular candidate system and threat model, survive adversarial evaluation, and pass through an institution capable of selecting and interpreting evaluators.

## Live branches

- One state permits only internal monitoring. The treaty survives because neither government actor violates it and both leaderships continue to accept imperfect evidence.
- A military or intelligence exemption creates a poorly observed capability channel without formal treaty withdrawal.
- Broad hosted inference preserves political support but accelerates algorithms or evasion enough to lower the effective training threshold.
- A false positive, concealed event, or selective report produces a serious bilateral verification crisis.
- Industry or national-security actors reshape evaluator selection and scope while preserving the formal institution.
- China or another actor uses cyber operations against suspected training projects, materially slowing work but increasing conflict and legitimacy costs.
- A Taiwan or other crisis interrupts inspections and command continuity even though both leaders retain explicit catastrophe beliefs.
- A genuine bounded AI-research improvement occurs and fizzles after moving one bottleneck; observers disagree whether it was evidence for or against near-term runaway.

## Tail but decision-relevant branches

- A covert or ambiguously authorized project crosses an unknown takeoff threshold during the first year.
- An existing system discovers an improvement route that operates inside one permitted inference episode, making the institution's normal response loop irrelevant.
- A technical insight sharply reduces the compute required for dangerous training and diffuses before controls adapt.
- The treaty undergoes explicit, rapid, bilateral abandonment despite both leaderships beginning the year committed to it.

Calling these “tail” is an ordinal judgment within the conditioning world, not a claim of negligible probability. Their consequence and the uncertainty over technical thresholds justify designing policy around them.

## Forecasts intentionally withheld

The atlas does not yet assign numeric first-year probabilities to collapse, covert training, RSI, capture, or success. Doing so responsibly requires:

1. a more exact initial legal and physical control package;
2. an inference-policy branch;
3. assumptions about existing model capability and algorithmic progress;
4. a definition of what counts as treaty failure versus degraded operation;
5. an elicitation process that preserves disagreement rather than averaging incompatible ontologies.

The next useful expert review is therefore about missing branches, wrong ordering, dominant mechanisms, and qualitative likelihood ranking—not percentage points.
