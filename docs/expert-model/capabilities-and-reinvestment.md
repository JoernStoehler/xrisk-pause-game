# Capabilities and cognitive reinvestment

## Current synthesis

Within an established paradigm, increasing effective compute tends to improve a broad, correlated capability factor while particular tasks cross thresholds at different times. A collection of monotone, often sigmoid-like task-response curves can therefore produce fairly smooth aggregate progress alongside a ragged frontier. Benchmark regressions are usually measurement noise, contamination changes, elicitation failures, or model/product changes—not evidence that underlying capability is independently redrawn each period.

This picture supports interpolation. It does not settle the asymptotics relevant to recursive self-improvement. Paradigm changes such as deep learning or transformers can alter the production function; benchmark saturation can conceal movement; and the capabilities needed for reinvestment may not be well measured by current suites.

## Live pathways to improvement

The atlas currently preserves at least four routes.

### Internal cognitive discovery

A system may discover, inside an inference episode, a better way to allocate attention, reason, remember, or search. The gain can be local to that context and leave little externally legible trace. Persistence requires some carrier: retained context, external memory, a communicable method, a scaffold change, or a trained successor.

### Explicit research and engineering

A system may develop an explicit theory, algorithm, experiment, or code change that improves later systems. This is more observable but requires experimental access, evaluation, implementation, and some route to new weights or scaffolds. It can proceed through a human–AI lab rather than an autonomous agent.

### Training-mediated inheritance

Improvements can be distilled, fine-tuned, or trained into successors. This supports cumulative reinvestment but consumes controlled resources and creates potential intervention points. The important quantities are not one “multiplier” but task-graph bottlenecks, serial depth, experiment throughput, evaluation reliability, inheritance fidelity, and wall-clock time.

### Economic and hardware reinvestment

AI labor can improve chips, systems, data centers, supply chains, software, and organizational productivity. This route is slower and more distributed in many worlds, but can broaden access and eventually make a nominal compute threshold obsolete.

## Runaway is not a one-stage property

An improvement episode can:

- fail to find a useful change;
- find a change that does not transfer;
- produce a bounded jump and encounter another bottleneck;
- improve research while evaluation or implementation remains limiting;
- shorten the next generation enough to outrun governance response;
- enable access acquisition or manipulation before another technical generation is needed;
- enter sustained recursive improvement.

Accordingly, the project should not treat “reproduction number above one” or a single effective-compute multiplier as a universal criterion. Useful diagnostics include successive generation times, preserved capacity, newly binding bottlenecks, access, observability, and the response time of the controlling institution.

## Why 1,000× effective compute does not answer the RSI question

Even a very large increase in effective compute may leave a qualitative prerequisite absent: long-horizon coherence, theory formation, experimental judgment, persistent memory, access to weights and hardware, or reliable inheritance. Conversely, RSI might not require the prerequisite observers expect. It could proceed through explicit ML research or through internally discovered cognition. A fixed multiplier therefore gives weak information about the capability profile at which recursive improvement begins unless the hypothesis connecting scale, profile, access, and reinvestment route is specified.

## Evidence and argument

- Scaling and benchmark research supports correlated progress and task-specific threshold/saturation behavior within observed regimes.
- Historical algorithmic discontinuities support retaining paradigm uncertainty, but do not provide an ASI takeoff base rate.
- Economic treatments of recursive improvement clarify resource substitution and feedback; they do not establish the empirical production function or alignment outcome.
- Jörn's review adds the distinction between context-local psychological improvement, explicit theory/code, and trained inheritance, plus the possibility of improvement that fizzles when another bottleneck binds.
- Yudkowsky's work supplies the default structural concern: sufficiently capable optimization can improve the processes producing further capability and can exploit routes that a narrow benchmark or “more of the same” forecast omits.

## Principal disagreements to preserve

1. Whether current scaling trends extrapolate far enough to reach the capabilities needed for reinvestment without a new paradigm.
2. Whether long-horizon coherence is a hard bottleneck, one route among several, or an artifact of present interfaces.
3. Whether the first important self-improvement is usually bounded or rapidly removes several bottlenecks.
4. Whether restricted inference is primarily a safety-research asset or a dominant channel for algorithmic and operational erosion of the pause.
5. Whether institutions receive usable warning before capability improvement compresses inside their response time.

## Pruning guidance for the game

Represent carrier scope, access, bottlenecks, generation time, and observability. Do not represent every cognitive task or ML research subfield. Several technical routes may share an abstract task-graph implementation if the player can still distinguish why an intervention works, why an episode fizzles, and why a later episode escapes control.
