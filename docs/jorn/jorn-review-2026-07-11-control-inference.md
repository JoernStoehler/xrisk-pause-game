## Reinvestment

Jörn endorsed the route/carrier/fizzle synthesis without a material correction.

## Punishment and proactive control

Punishment and legal deterrence can cause many actors to back off, especially
actors who are not willing to gamble on immediately building ASI in order to
take over the world. The latter plan is not a plausible path to successful
control; under the default model it builds an unsafe ASI and kills everyone.

Therefore “punishment is too late” is too broad. The corrected distinction is:

- punishment, liability, criminal enforcement and expected sanctions can prevent
  many ordinary or incremental violations before they are attempted;
- they do not robustly cover actors willing to attempt a fast decisive run,
  actors who expect impunity, or runs that finish before detection/enforcement;
- physical custody, direct interruption and other proactive controls are the
  backstop for that catastrophic residual class.

The game should represent deterrence and physical prevention as complementary,
not as a choice between a useless punishment regime and a useful control regime.

## Degrees of prevention and political willingness

The relevant policy question is not whether evasion becomes “impossible.”
Controls reduce the set of feasible actors, resource configurations and routes,
raise cost and operational exposure, increase warning time, and sometimes make a
particular route physically unavailable.

Jörn would prefer controls extending even to old consumer gaming GPUs—for
example, disabling ten-year-old hardware through a global Stuxnet-like cyber
operation—but judges such action politically unlikely to be available. His
explanation is insufficient willingness to protect against the risk, whether
because decision-makers do not care enough about future people/their children or
do not take the risk seriously enough.

This separates:

1. the technically preferred risk-minimizing control surface;
2. the politically feasible control surface; and
3. residual dangerous resources after the feasible policy.

The global-malware example is a policy-preference/extreme-control branch, not a
claim about its legality, operational reliability, side effects, or current
political feasibility.

## Inference and a dual-use efficiency regime shift

Jörn is uncertain how much capability progress occurs with or without GPT-6-
level inference. A salient hypothesis is that interpretability-like research
locates internal blockers that training failed to solve and turns them into much
larger algorithmic-efficiency progress.

Many researchers have tried and failed to find such blockers. This is evidence
against easy success, but not decisive if:

- no capability-relevant research ban exists;
- companies redirect research from larger training runs toward efficiency; and
- effective human research labor on the problem rises by roughly 10×.

Under that package, Jörn gives roughly **25%** to a large breakthrough/regime
shift. Restricting or banning inference from GPT-5.0-and-larger systems reduces
the corresponding risk to roughly **10%**, not zero. The residual route is that
roughly 70B open-weight models may already be sufficient for the relevant
research assistance; they run on consumer hardware and sometimes CPU-only.

The current intended magnitude is a transition to roughly **2× algorithmic-
efficiency progress per year**, which compounds to about (2^{10} \approx 1,000)
over a ten-year pause. This may erase the safety margin unless covered compute
thresholds can be reduced by a similar factor. Political unwillingness to ban or
disable consumer hardware can prevent that adaptation.

## Semantics to confirm before calibration

- Whether 25% and 10% are probabilities that a persistent 2×/year efficiency
  regime begins within one year, or probabilities of at least one one-off 2×
  advance during the year. The compounding example suggests the former, which is
  the provisional interpretation here.
- Whether the 25% condition already includes the stated 10× labor redirection or
  whether labor scale is one route contributing to it.
- What GPT-5.0-and-larger means operationally when models differ in architecture,
  access, tools and capability profile.
- Whether the 10% residual conditions on existing open-weight copies being
  impossible to remove, or merely on the proposed policy leaving them untouched.

Until confirmed, use 25%→10% as an approximate comparative judgment and model
the 2×/year regime as a named structural branch rather than independent yearly
2× events.

## Breaches are hazard transitions, not terminal outcomes

Jörn corrected the vague claim that treaty failure merely precedes an unsafe
decisive system. Distinct failures can worsen the state while leaving recovery
possible:

- leaked capability insights increase algorithmic progress or lower future
  resource requirements, but do not by themselves kill everyone;
- a dangerous training run can start and then be interrupted after a delay. It
  is fatal only in worlds where the system finds and executes a sufficiently
  fast internal RSI/takeover path before interruption; otherwise the stop works,
  perhaps without observers learning how close the run came;
- a signatory such as the US can leave or suspend the treaty and later re-enter
  before anyone builds superintelligence. Restoring bans, monitoring and factual
  control will be difficult, but remains worth attempting.

The engine must therefore preserve elapsed time, hidden technical progress,
observed evidence, degraded controls, and available recovery actions after a
breach. Leakage, run initiation, interruption, withdrawal, re-entry, unsafe
system creation, loss of control and extinction are separate state transitions.
